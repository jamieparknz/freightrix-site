Freight emissions: the global picture and what it means for Australian freight

Your customers, their banks and their overseas buyers are all starting to ask about emissions. This briefing sets out the key numbers, the rules that are coming through the supply chain, and the first steps any carrier can take this year.

The global picture

Transport produced 8.7 Gt CO2e of direct emissions in 2019. That was 23% of global energy related CO2 emissions. Road vehicles made up 70% of transport emissions.

Trucks and buses are fewer than 8% of vehicles, excluding two and three wheelers. Yet they produce more than 35% of direct CO2 from road transport, according to the IEA.

Demand keeps growing. The International Transport Forum expects global freight demand to roughly double by 2050 under current policies.

Australia's numbers

Australia emitted 455.6 Mt CO2e in the year to March 2026. Transport was 22.1% of the national inventory, or about 100.6 Mt CO2e.

Transport emissions rose 0.2% over that year. The main driver was a 1.5% increase in road transport diesel use.

Heavy vehicles (trucks and buses) make up 24% of transport emissions, about 22 Mt CO2e, according to the Climate Change Authority. Cars and light commercial vehicles make up 60%.

Global rules moving through supply chains

Most of these rules do not apply to an Australian trucking business directly. They apply to the shippers, importers and lenders you work with, and they flow down to carriers as data requests.

  • IMO Net Zero Framework (shipping). Approved in draft in April 2025, but the adoption session was adjourned for one year in October 2025. It is not in force. The adjourned session is due to resume on 4 December 2026, subject to confirmation by MEPC 85. The IMO target is net zero shipping emissions by or around 2050.
  • EU ETS for shipping. Covers cargo and passenger ships of 5,000 GT and above. Ships surrender allowances for 70% of 2025 emissions in 2026, and 100% from 2027. Voyages between the EU and a port outside it count at 50%.
  • EU CBAM. The definitive period started on 1 January 2026. EU importers of goods such as steel, cement and fertilisers must now buy certificates for embedded emissions.
  • EU CSRD. The Omnibus I changes narrowed mandatory reporting to companies with more than EUR 450 million turnover and an average of 1,000 employees. Member States must transpose the changes by 19 March 2027.
  • ISSB IFRS S2. The global baseline for climate disclosure, effective for periods from 1 January 2024. Australia's AASB S2 is based on it.
  • ISO 14083:2023 and the GLEC Framework. ISO 14083, published in March 2023, sets a common method for transport chain emissions. Smart Freight Centre's GLEC Framework is the foundation of ISO 14083 and its practical guide.

Australian rules and programs

  • Mandatory climate reporting (AASB S2). Group 1 reports for financial years starting on or after 1 January 2025. Group 2 starts on or after 1 July 2026. Group 3 starts on or after 1 July 2027.
  • Why shippers will ask you. Large companies must disclose material risks in their value chain, including Scope 3. ASIC says many will use estimates and industry averages. Carriers that can supply real data stand out. The Government is also consulting on raising the large proprietary company thresholds to $100 million revenue and $50 million assets.
  • NGER. Corporate groups report if they reach 50,000 t CO2e (Scope 1 and 2) or 200 TJ of energy. The facility threshold is 25,000 t CO2e or 100 TJ.
  • Safeguard Mechanism. Covers facilities above 100,000 t CO2e a year. In 2023 to 24 there were 219 facilities, including some in transport, producing around 31% of national emissions.
  • New Vehicle Efficiency Standard. Applies to new vehicles up to 4.5 t GVM only. Heavy trucks are not covered.
  • Cleaner Fuels Program. Up to $1.1 billion in production incentives over 10 years for low carbon liquid fuels such as renewable diesel. ARENA is the delivery agency. It is not yet open for applications.
  • Truck width. Safer Freight Vehicles with set safety features can be 2.55 m wide, up from 2.5 m. This applies to rigid trucks and prime movers, not trailers.
  • NSW zero emission heavy vehicle access. From 27 May 2026 an ongoing notice allows eligible zero emission trucks up to 8 t on a single steer axle and 18.5 t on a tandem drive axle, up to 26 t GVM, on mapped roads. Telematics with Smart OBM is required.

What to do now

Start with fuel. Diesel burned in your own trucks is your Scope 1. To your customers it is Scope 3.

Worked example using the National Greenhouse Accounts Factors 2026 for heavy duty vehicles, Euro IV or higher. Diesel has 38.6 GJ per kL. The Scope 1 factor is 70.37 kg CO2e per GJ, about 2.72 kg CO2e per litre. The upstream Scope 3 factor is 17.3 kg CO2e per GJ, about 0.67 kg per litre.

A fleet using 100,000 litres (100 kL) a year: 100 x 38.6 x 70.37 / 1,000 = 271.6 t CO2e Scope 1. Upstream fuel adds 100 x 38.6 x 17.3 / 1,000 = 66.8 t CO2e. Total well to wheel is about 338 t CO2e.

  • Record litres per vehicle, per month, from fuel cards and bulk tank logs.
  • Record kilometres and tonnes carried per job, so you can report tonne kilometres.
  • Calculate with the GLEC Framework and ISO 14083 so your figures match what shippers use.
  • Use the latest NGA Factors each year and note which edition you used.
  • Keep evidence. Shippers' reports are moving to assurance.
