Fuel levy education

How a fuel levy works

A fuel levy is a rise and fall adjustment on an agreed freight rate. It moves the fuel part of your cost up or down with the diesel price, and only that part.

Fuel levy weighting

The share of your total operating cost that is fuel. A linehaul fleet burning a lot of diesel per hour of driver time sits higher than a metro operation with more waiting and handling. Work it out from your own numbers. Published examples vary: Cochrane's in South Australia publishes a fuel factor of 27 per cent.

Base price

The diesel price built into your current rates, set when you last agreed or reviewed them.

Current price

The benchmark price now, usually an average over the review period. Many carriers use AIP terminal gate prices because anyone can check them. Team Global Express indexes its surcharge to the AIP national average and updates it weekly.

Difference

Current price minus base price, in cents per litre and as a percentage of the base.

Fuel levy calculation

Levy % = (current price − base price) ÷ base price × fuel weighting. Example: base 220 c/L, current 242 c/L, weighting 30 per cent. (242 − 220) ÷ 220 = 10 per cent. 10 per cent × 30 per cent = a 3.0 per cent levy on the base rate.

GST and fuel tax credits

Most levies use prices excluding GST because GST is claimed back. Some deduct the fuel tax credit as well. From 3 August 2026 the credit for heavy vehicles on public roads is 21.3 c/L: excise of 53.7 c/L less the road user charge of 32.4 c/L. Say which basis you use in your contract.

The rules

The Fair Work Commission's Road Transport Contractual Chain Order on fuel cost recovery started operating again on 23 November 2026. Check whether it applies to your contracts.
